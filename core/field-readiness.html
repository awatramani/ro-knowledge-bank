Module 3: Launch Prep

Field Readiness

The bridge between planning and execution. Field readiness is where your survey instrument, field team, logistics, and monitoring architecture come together—and are stress-tested before scale.

Every rupee spent on preparation during this phase saves five during execution. Too often, projects rush from training directly into full-scale launch. The first few days should be seen as input days, opportunities to find broken skip patterns, iron out field coordination inconsistencies, and correct communication flows. The inconsistencies and deviations you encounter in the first few days are NORMAL, it is a part of the process, and are bound to happen, there are nuances that field team will learn by making errors. This section on Field readiness is designed to prevent these failures before they scale.

The Field Readiness Equation

Right people, trained well × Tested, digitized and version locked Instrument × Clear Expectations around reporting and tracking processes × Working conditions and team morale × Live Monitoring and your own involvement = Field Ready

If any one multiplier is zero, the product is zero. A brilliant survey instrument with an inadequately trained team produces bad data. A well-trained team without pre-planned route plans results into inefficiencies. Field readiness requires all five.

What does 'Field Ready' actually mean?

A project is only field-ready when these five pillars are validated through real-world testing:

Pillar What It Covers Validated Through
People Hiring timeline, quality, team structure, role clarity,  Screening, Mock interviews, training evaluations, field training performance
Instrument Survey logic, skip patterns, translation accuracy, response codes Pilot surveys with 25-40 diverse respondents
Processes Daily rhythms, reporting flows, escalation protocols, handovers 2-3 pilot days simulating real field conditions
Systems Tablets, SurveyCTO, GPS, data sync, attendance tracking Full end-to-end data flow test from field to server
Monitoring Supervision frameworks, daily survey efficiency and quality, back-check logistics Daily review
🗣️ The ₹4.2 Lakh Lesson in Jharkhand

RA Neha was managing a 12-surveyor team for an education RCT in Ranchi. Due to time crunch and stressed timelines, she had to skip the mock interviews and field practice during the field training and relied mostly on the rigorous time she spent on instrument walkthrough, and launched directly after instrument training. By Day 2, critical problems emerged: a broken skip pattern recorded wrong data,  Supervisors missed allocating H/H efficiently, daily debriefs lacked structure and motivation.

Cost: 340 surveys required re-visits ( costing an additional ₹1.2 lakh), 2 weeks of overall delay, and ₹3 lakh in extended field staff contracts. A 3-day pilot would have caught everything.
Lesson: 'Jaldi karna hai toh pehle theek se taiyaari karo' (If you want speed, first prepare properly).

The Field Readiness Journey

Field Readiness is a sequence of deliberate steps. The journey begins with hiring the right people and ends with a fully operational field machine that self-corrects in real time. Go through the sections below for detailed review.

1️⃣ Hiring Field Staff & Core Team

Your field team is your data collection engine. Every data point you analyse began as a conversation between a surveyor and a respondent. The quality of that conversation depends entirely on who we hire.

  • Sourcing: Strategies that the Survey Coordinator will deploy to source candidates, in addition to reaching out to suitable candidates who are available
  • Screening: Screening architecture to bring in the right candidates
  • Team Structure: Sizing, supervisor-to-surveyor ratios, and bench strength.
  • Validation: Any Field Staff getting a contract after training is assumed to be fit for the job based on your rigorous review of their skills. 
🚩 The Cost of a Bad Hire One surveyor who fabricates 1 survey per day across a 30-day deployment produces 30 fraudulent SURVEYS. At ₹500/survey for back-check remediation, that single deviation and inability to catch these deviations early costs ₹30,000, plus weeks of team disruption and potential IRB complications.

2️⃣ Piloting and Pre-Launch Activities

Piloting is the single most undervalued activity in survey operations. It is a full dress rehearsal where you test the instrument, people, processes, and systems simultaneously.

✅ The 6 Pillars of a Strong Pilot
  1. Test with real respondents, not team members, in working condition that mimic the complexity of real survey day
  2. Cover ALL demographic edge cases (joint families, migrants).
  3. Run the full daily cycle: briefing → field → debrief → sync.
  4. Populate your monitoring dashboard with pilot data.
  5. Time every survey, track the median, not the mean.
  6. Debrief every surveyor.

🗣️ The Skip Pattern Nobody Tested

During a hurried 1-day pilot in Muzaffarpur, the team tested only 5 surveys, all with nuclear families. On Day 7 of full launch, joint families with 8-12 members triggered a logic error.

Cost: 280 corrupted surveys, ₹1.8 lakh in re-survey costs, 11-day delay.
Lesson: Pilot with 25-40 respondents covering ALL demographic edge cases.

3️⃣ Survey Launch and Initial Days

The first two weeks are when habits form, norms solidify, and problems either get caught early or become entrenched. A project that establishes discipline in the first 14 days maintains it. A project that drifts and continues without addressing systematically either plays the catching up game or  rarely recovers/discovers.

4️⃣ Google Maps to Track Productivity

With evolving technology and times, a field plan isn't just a printed list of village names. Tech platforms like Google My Maps are easy and efficient ways to put together real-time field maps with colour-coded pins showing treatment arms, team assignments, travel routes, and completion status, that is a field plan.

  • Treatment Arms: Colour-coded pins prevent contamination.
  • Team Assignments: Visual workload balancing identifies travel inefficiencies.
  • Travel Routes: Plotted paths optimize fuel costs and reduce backtracking.
  • Completion Status: Pin styles change (hollow → filled) for real-time progress.
🗣️ How Maps Saved 3 Weeks in Udaipur

RA Meera generated her field assignments using Stata, logically clustering the sampled villages by their official Gram Panchayat (GP) and Block codes. Mathematically, it was the optimal way to distribute the workload, so she assigned all 15 villages in "Block X" to Team A.

Before finalizing the plan, Meera uploaded the GPS coordinates of the sampled households into Google My Maps. The visual layout immediately revealed a critical issue that the spreadsheet hid: Block X was bisected by a major river, and the nearest bridge was 40 kilometers south.
While Villages 1 and 2 shared the exact same administrative codes, driving between them would force Team A into a 2-hour detour. Furthermore, the map revealed that two villages in neighboring "Block Y" were actually located on the same highway just 5 kilometers from Team A's route.
Instead of strictly following administrative boundaries, Meera reorganized the assignments geographically. She grouped the trans-river villages with Team B (who were already operating on that side of the water) and absorbed the highway-adjacent Block Y villages into Team A's route. This 10-minute visual check saved the field teams hours of dead transit time and fuel costs.

Result: Daily travel dropped from 45km to 18km per team. Saved ₹25000 to 50000 in travel costs and gave teams 1.5 extra survey hours daily.
Lesson: Always plot before you deploy. Geography is the primary planning variable.

5️⃣ Digital Attendance Tracking

Moving away from paper registers to automated attendance, leave management, and live productivity dashboards starting from Day 1 ensures absolute accountability and transparent payroll processing.

The Core Strategy

The "Buffer" Rule

Hiring for the field is unique. You aren't just looking for competence; you are looking for resilience. In short-term projects, the biggest risk is attrition, where staff do not show up after training. 

The 20% Rule: If you need 20 surveyors, train 25. If all 25 stay, you finish data collection early. If 5 drop out, your timeline is safe.

Pro Tip: The Language Switch Don't just interview in one language. During the screening call, switch languages mid-sentence (e.g., English to Hindi/Tamil). If they stumble, they will likely struggle with translation in the field.

Contracting Guidelines

90-Day Contract Policy

All field staff, including Project Associates, Field Supervisors, and Surveyors, are hired on a short-term, fixed-term, non-extendable 90-day contract.

The 7-Day Cooling-Off Rule When a contract expires, a new contract can be raised only after a mandatory 7-day cooling-off period. This is non-negotiable and must be factored into your staffing plan. If your survey extends beyond 90 days, plan the contract renewal cycle in advance so there is no gap in coverage.

Fast-Track Screening Protocol

The 10-Domain Screening Architecture

Screening is a confidence-seeking conversation, not a formal examination. The SC assesses candidates across 10 core domains using a standardised 3-point scoring scale.

1Basic Qualification & Identity

Age, education, ID documents, and verification of stated credentials.

2Language & Communication

Fluency in local language. Mid-sentence language switch test. Clarity and confidence in verbal communication.

3Listening & Instruction Following

Can they repeat instructions accurately? Do they ask clarifying questions or guess?

4Reading Comprehension

Read a short passage aloud and answer questions. Tests literacy and comprehension simultaneously.

5Attention to Detail & Numeracy

Simple arithmetic, spotting errors in a mock form, and reading a measurement correctly.

6Digital & Device Readiness

Can they operate a basic Android tablet? Do they understand mobile data sync?

7Travel Readiness & Availability

Full availability for the entire duration including Sundays. Comfortable with overnight stays in field locations.

8Work Ethic & Problem Solving

How do they handle a respondent who refuses? What do they do when a tablet dies mid-survey?

9Values & Safeguarding

Understanding of confidentiality, informed consent, and how to handle sensitive disclosures.

10Confidence & Presence

Do they maintain eye contact? Can they explain research purpose clearly to a skeptical respondent?

The Person Behind Every Data Point

The surveyor is the crucial bridge between the organization and the communities where data is collected. Every data point analysed by researchers began as a conversation between a surveyor and a respondent. The quality of that conversation, and therefore the quality of your data, depends entirely on who you hire and how well you train them.

Whether working in the streets of old Jaipur or the remote villages of Bihar, the surveyor's presence, empathy, and attention to detail determines whether the data reflects reality or manufactures fiction.

Piloting & Pre-Launch

Piloting is the critical stage where theory meets reality. It is not just about testing the questionnaire but about stress-testing the entire survey system in real-world conditions. A successful pilot phase should leave you confident that the survey can be launched at scale without surprises.

The Human Dimension of Piloting

A pilot is not only a test of tools and protocols, but also an opportunity to understand the human realities of field implementation. The individuals who will eventually carry out large-scale data collection are the backbone of the process, and their ability to work effectively depends on the environment and conditions in which they operate along with the applicability and appropriateness of the instrument and the processes that can enable them to carry out their work effectively.

This means that assessing the physical demands and logistical constraints that field staff will encounter is as critical as assessing if the instrument works, if there are questions that are not received the way we want them to, the expected cooperation and smooth functioning will be a far-fetched dream.  Teams should take a critical view of travel distances, the complexity of routes, terrain challenges, and the impact of geographic spread and be thoroughly informed about the intricate working conditions in which field staff will operate to get a reasonable assessment of the daily workload and expected productivity. One should keep room for less experienced surveyors and other field staff to lean and grow, we need to accept the fact that there indeed is a learning curve when it comes to mastering the art of flawless surveying and survey management and that people will take time to reach to that level at different pace. Long travel times, poor connectivity, unpredictable transport options, or difficult terrain can all impact data quality and enumerator's motivation, morale, confidence and overall well-being, if not anticipated early.

Ultimately, the aim is to pre-empt fatigue, bottlenecks, and operational risks, laying the groundwork for a data collection phase that is not only efficient but also humane and sustainable.

Key Objectives of Piloting

FOcus & methodology

Comprehension & Flow

Finalize and test the questionnaire across multiple modules. Assess clarity, flow, and skip logic specifically in the local language.

Respondent Burden

Evaluate the comfort level and burden on respondents. Understand the exact time required for each module under real field conditions across different set of staff leading it based on experience.

Logistical Friction

Assess real-life logistical challenges: travel times, location access, and realistic scheduling limits for enumerators.

Protocol Tuning

Fine-tune implementation protocols required for upcoming surveyor training and established realistic field monitoring/supervisory and reporting structures.

Team Feedback Loop

Capture field team feedback and concerns during daily debriefs and rapidly absorb these insights into the final protocol updates.

Ethical Integrity

Ensure ethical procedures and consent protocols actually work in practice without intimidating or confusing the respondent.

Operating Philosophy

Core Principles of the Crash Test

If your pilot does not break anything, you did not pilot hard enough.

Mimic Final Conditions

Pilot in the same type of setting where the main data collection will occur. Urban slums, remote villages, classrooms, or government offices all carry unique constraints.

Sample Smartly

Choose respondents across key categories (e.g., varying age, location, and literacy levels) to test the range and boundary cases of your instrument.

Pilot Everything

Test devices, translations, audio consents, the sampling process, supervisor debrief protocols, and physical consent tracking sheets.

Absorb & Adapt

Use daily debriefs to identify friction points. Involve the core team in every conversation and be willing to rewrite protocols based on field feedback.

Survey Launch & Gestation Phase

The survey launch is more than just Day 1 of fieldwork. It is the crucial transition period where your project's blueprint—its people, plans, and protocols—comes to life.

For field teams and RAs, this is the moment to ensure everything is lined up, clearly communicated, and fully functional. The first 10 to 15 days are used to build momentum, establish unbreakable routines, and surface early operational lessons.

Key Pillars of a Successful Launch

1. Clarity for Field Staff

At launch, staff must have absolute clarity on roles, contracting details, daily routines, working conditions, and payment terms (stipend/DA frequency). There is no room for ambiguity.

Action: Distribute Field Manuals and R&R documents in advance. Review them extensively during training and reiterate them on launch morning. Call out on the key R&R and expectations

Field Scenario A surveyor arrives on Day 1 confused about whether transport is reimbursed based on actual bills that she should collect or is a flat allowance. They spend the first two hours arguing with the supervisor instead of conducting surveys. Clear payment terms prevent Day 1 friction.

2. Logistics & Field Readiness

Every element of planning must be finalized: Start times, pickup points, whether taxi routes are fixed, lunch arrangements, emergency contacts, and device charging protocols.

Action: Document all of this in a Field Logistics SOP. The Project specific 'Field Logistics SOP' with clear expectations and timeline is an extremely useful resource. Keep laminated hard copies inside every team vehicle.

Field Scenario The team reaches a remote village, but the tablets are at 15% battery because the charging protocol wasn't enforced the night before. The entire day is lost. Logistics dictate data flow.

Reporting & Debriefing Systems

The launch week must introduce the culture of daily field reflection.

  • Decide who leads daily debriefs (Usually PA schedules and facilitates it along with RA and SC (joining remotely is not at site).
  • Maintain a Debrief Tracker to capture and record key themes.
  • Encourage open conversations: what’s working, what’s frustrating? Make efforts to open up people, this is where RAs and SC need to add value, they need to instill confidence into people 
Pro Tip Make the debriefs for the first 10 days mandatory and highly structured, this builds discipline and trust early on.

Monitoring & Escalation Setup

Finalize exactly how survey progress and emergencies will be tracked.

  • Share protocols, point of contact and escalation ladder: device failures, cab delays, respondent refusals, community issues, or safety concerns.

  • Assign point persons for Daily Attendance, Bike logs, Field Staff Outstation Travel Tracker, Vendor Issues, Logistics Tracking (PA/FM), and Backcheck team (RA).

RA Launch Checklist & Tips

  • Create a launch kick-off pack: Include printed field plans, route maps, supervisor contacts, attendance formats, and team rosters.
  • Printer Access: Ensure at least one field team member knows a local shop for emergency forms/logs.
  • Schedule Yourself: Work with RO to build a launch week schedule that includes active in-field time splits between you and the SC for the coming weeks and ensure the unpredictability of the visit remains in place. 2. Creating & Importing Your Map

Google My Maps is a free, browser-based tool that lets you create custom maps with pins, routes, and multiple layers. Follow these exact steps to load your dummy data.

Step 1: Upload to Google Sheets First

  1. Go to mymaps.google.com and click '+ CREATE A NEW MAP'.

The most critical visual distinction is Treatment vs. Control. You cannot apply two color schemes to the same layer, so we will create distinct layers for different views.

Step 3: Color-Code Treatment Arms 

  1. In the layer panel, click 'Uniform style' → Change to 'Style by data column'.
  2. Select 'treatment_status'.
  3. Click the paint bucket next to 'Treatment' → Choose 🟢 GREEN (#34A853).
  4. Click the paint bucket next to 'Control' → Choose 🔴 RED (#EA4335).

Step 4: Create Team Assignment Layer

  2. Import the same Google Sheet into this new layer.

This is the most operationally powerful layer. For each day, you create a sub-layer showing only that day's targets for each biker. We use the 3+3 Model: 3 primary targets and 3 buffer households to ensure no surveyor has a zero-productivity day.

Why Digital Attendance Matters

From Paper to GPS-Verified Tracking

Paper attendance registers are easy to fabricate, impossible to verify remotely, and provide zero spatial intelligence. The digital attendance system captures four GPS coordinates per day per staff member, creating a verifiable trail of field presence. This transforms attendance from a trust-based system into a data-driven one.

The system serves three stakeholders: Project Associates gain real-time visibility into team deployment, Research Associates can correlate attendance patterns with data quality, and Research Operations can benchmark productivity across projects.

The Accountability Chain Every morning a surveyor starts their day, the app captures their home location (L1). When they reach the field, it captures the first survey site (L2). When they finish fieldwork, it captures the last site (L3). When they return home, it captures the end point (L4). Four coordinates, four verifiable moments, zero ambiguity.

Remote Survey Operations:
The Complete Playbook

A practitioner's guide for PAs implementing large-scale telephone surveys end-to-end. From contact database preparation through deployment, quality management, and close-out — covering CATI methodology, Exotel integration, remote training architecture, and daily operations.

1
Strategic Overview: What Is a Remote Survey?

Understanding Phone-Based Data Collection (CATI)

A remote survey — also called CATI (Computer-Assisted Telephone Interviewing) — is a mode where the entire survey process happens over phone calls. Surveyors work from their homes or local offices, not centralised call centres. The instrument is administered via SurveyCTO, and calls are routed through Exotel, which masks surveyor personal numbers.

Remote surveys became mainstream post-COVID-19 but their value extends beyond pandemic contingency: faster deployment (no travel logistics), lower per-survey cost, ability to reach vast geographies simultaneously. However, they introduce three critical risks absent in face-to-face collection:

Verification Risk

Cannot visually confirm respondent identity or household context. Must rely on verbal verification questions.

Engagement Risk

Phone disconnections, distractions, limited rapport. Respondents can hang up with zero social cost.

Visibility Risk

Supervisors cannot observe surveyor technique. Requires stronger systems for quality monitoring (call recordings, HFCs).

When to Use Remote vs In-PersonUse remote when: contact databases exist, instrument is under 30 min, questions don't require visual observation, geography is too dispersed for field teams. Use in-person when: measurement needs physical tools, respondent literacy is very low, or community trust requires face-to-face rapport.
2
The Five Pillars of a Successful Remote Survey
📱

Contact Database Readiness

The single most important input. Numbers must be current (under 6 months old), correctly formatted, consent-verified. Expect 20–40% invalid. The RA must audit the database before anything else begins.

🎓

Systematic Remote Training

3–7 day training via video conferencing. Smaller batches (8–12 per session). Move from protocols → instrument → mock calls → supervised pilot → deployment. Never skip the "no forms" conceptual walkthrough.

📞

Telephony Integration

Exotel integrated with SurveyCTO. Respondent numbers masked. Calls auto-logged. Recharge managed by A&F. If balance runs out mid-call, the call disconnects — monitor credits daily.

📊

Active Quality Management

Real-time monitoring: call recordings, high-frequency checks, daily debriefs, immediate corrective action. Flag short calls, excessive "don't know" responses, and skip logic violations.

🔒

PII Protection (Non-Negotiable)

Respondent phone numbers must NEVER appear in WhatsApp groups, tracking sheets, or unsecured channels. Exotel masking is the first line of defense. Violations can shut down the entire project.

This is the PA's operational playbook. Each week has a primary objective, specific daily tasks, and common pitfalls to avoid.

Objective: Build a surveyor roster with verified equipment. Post job openings (WhatsApp groups, local colleges, previous surveyor networks). Objective: Finalize all training materials and test platform with selected candidates. The Survey Call Process: Step-by-Step for Surveyors

Every Call Follows This Exact Structure

1
Before You Dial

Open SurveyCTO → correct form. Check network (2+ bars). Put on headphones, test mic. Review respondent info. Take a breath — sound calm and professional.

2
Making the Call

Tap the phone icon in SurveyCTO → Exotel dials automatically. Wait 25–30 seconds before hanging up. Many rural respondents take time to reach their phone.

3
Introduction Script (Memorise Verbatim)

"Namaste, my name is [NAME] calling from [ORG]. May I speak with [RESPONDENT]?" → Verify identity → Explain purpose → State duration → Obtain consent.

4
Respondent Verification (Critical)

Ask open-ended: "What is your name?" — do NOT ask "Is this [NAME]?" (people say yes to be polite even when it's not them). Verify 2–3 identifying details.

5
Conducting the Survey

Read questions EXACTLY as written. Read ALL answer options. Speak slowly. Never suggest answers. Follow skip logic (trust SurveyCTO). Record answers immediately. Remain neutral — no surprise, agreement, or disagreement.

6
Closing the Call

"Thank you for your time. Your responses are very valuable. If you have questions, contact [SUPERVISOR] at [NUMBER]." Submit the form. Sync immediately.

Handling Difficult Moments

Inconsistent Answers"Earlier you mentioned 3 children, now you're saying 2 attend school. Just to confirm — how many school-age children do you have?" Gently clarify, never accuse.
Respondent Gets Emotional"I understand this is difficult. Take your time. Would you like a short break?" Show empathy. If they want to stop, respect it — mark partial, offer callback.
Call Drops (Network)Wait 30 seconds → call back immediately → "I'm sorry we got disconnected. Can we continue?" SurveyCTO should have saved responses to the last question.
Respondent Busy Mid-Survey"We've completed X of Y questions. May I call back? When is convenient?" Record callback time, save partial survey.
Asks for Personal Number"I'm calling through a research line. For questions, please contact my supervisor at [NUMBER]." Never give your personal number.
Respondent Becomes AbusiveStay calm: "I understand. Your participation is voluntary. I'll end the call now. Thank you." Hang up. Report to supervisor immediately.
